The Problem

Where Campus Energy Goes to Waste

Traditional energy systems use static thresholds — they can't tell the difference between a packed auditorium at peak heat and an empty classroom at 2 AM. Both might draw 50 kW, but only one is waste.

💡

Lights Left On Overnight

Classrooms and offices with projectors, computers, and lighting systems running through the night and on weekends with zero occupancy.

❄️

AC Running in Empty Halls

Air conditioning systems running at full cooling capacity in completely empty lecture halls — often triggered by timers that don't account for schedule changes.

🔬

Lab Equipment During Holidays

Chemistry and biology laboratory equipment operating continuously during semester breaks, consuming kilowatts with no productive output.

📅

Weekend Overconsumption

Server rooms, refrigeration units, and standby loads running at weekday levels through Saturday and Sunday with minimal to no occupancy.

Methodology

How the Waste Detective Works

A five-step ML pipeline that combines physics-informed feature engineering with contextual anomaly detection.

01
📡

Collect Telemetry

Gather hourly campus data: occupancy headcount, outdoor temperature & humidity, timestamp, and actual power consumption (kW) per building zone.

02
⚙️

Engineer Features

Create physics-driven features: cooling degree demand, business-hours flag, cyclical sin/cos hour encoding, weekend indicators, and one-hot building encoding.

03
🤖

Predict Expected Load

XGBoost Regressor (R² = 0.9043) trained on chronological 80% split predicts what power consumption should be given context — no future data leakage.

04
🔍

Detect Waste Contextually

Flag an incident only when: Residual ≥ 10 kW AND Occupancy ≤ 5 people. This dual condition eliminates false alarms from legitimate high-load events.

05
₹

Estimate Financial Impact

Excess Energy = Residual kW × 1 hour. Financial Loss = Excess kWh × ₹8.50/kWh tariff. Actionable estimates, transparently calculated.

About This Project

WattWise is an academic Machine Learning mini-project. The dataset is synthetically simulated — not live campus sensor data. Ground-truth anomaly labels exist solely for evaluation and are never passed as features.

The system demonstrates a methodology for detecting energy waste in institutional buildings. It does not make guarantees about real-world savings or integrate with live IoT systems.

Academic Simulation Notice: All figures shown are derived from synthetic telemetry generated using thermodynamics equations and occupancy distributions. Results demonstrate methodology, not verified campus readings.
